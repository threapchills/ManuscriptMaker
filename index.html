A note on honesty. Thorn and eth were never cleanly divided even by the scribes who used them, and no rule can perfectly predict which one a word wants. This one leans on a dictionary of the awkward cases and a fallback: a th sitting between two vowels is soft, everything else is hard. Eng and yogh get the same treatment. It is right the vast majority of the time and will occasionally write something a monk would frown at.

It also cannot see the seams inside compound words, so the odd lighthouſe comes out with a thorn where two words merely collided. The scribes made worse mistakes.
